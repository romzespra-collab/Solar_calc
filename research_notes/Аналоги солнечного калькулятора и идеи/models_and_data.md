## Q1. Weather data APIs: PVGIS (all tools), Open-Meteo, NASA POWER, Forecast.Solar — endpoints, parameters, formats, limits, licensing

### Takeaway
PVGIS 5.3 `seriescalc` (multi-year hourly 2005-2023, SARAH-3) and `tmy` give exactly what an hour-by-hour simulator needs (GHI/DNI/DHI or POA components, T2m, WS10m), and `printhorizon` gives a free 360° horizon profile; Open-Meteo's archive/satellite APIs add snowfall/snow depth and newer years but are free only for non-commercial use, and its built-in tilted irradiance is a plain isotropic model, so the calculator should download components and transpose them itself.

### Inferences
- For a desktop app: fetch PVGIS `seriescalc` once per location with `angle=0&components=1` (then `Gb(i)` + `Gd(i)` on a horizontal plane = beam-horizontal and DHI, so GHI = Gb + Gd and DNI = Gb/sin(H_sun)), or request `tmy`, which returns GHI/DNI/DHI directly; cache the result locally (a 19-year hourly series is ~166k rows, roughly 10-20 MB of JSON). The `tmy` endpoint is the cheapest single call for a "typical year"; `seriescalc` over 2005-2023 is the right input for worst-year / LOLP statistics.
- The SARAH "instantaneous at HH:MM" vs ERA5/Open-Meteo "backward hourly average" timestamp conventions must be handled, otherwise a ±30 min shift biases east/west arrays and the morning/evening battery balance.
- Open-Meteo is the only free source in this list giving **snowfall and snow depth** at hourly resolution (needed for snow models, Q6); its non-commercial clause matters if the calculator is ever sold.
- Don't use Open-Meteo's `global_tilted_irradiance` for tilted panels if the program implements Perez — request `direct_radiation`/`diffuse_radiation` (or DNI) instead.

## Q2. Realistic sequences of cloudy/sunny days for loss-of-load probability (Markov chains, Aguiar et al.)

### Takeaway
Replacing "average day + 3 weather types" with a real multi-year hourly series (PVGIS `seriescalc`) or a synthetic Markov-chain sequence of daily clearness index is the single largest accuracy improvement for battery sizing: averaging hides multi-day dull spells, and in our test the average-day method reported 0% unmet load where real hourly weather gave 1-9%.

### Cited Findings
- Aguiar, Collares-Pereira & Conde, "Simple procedure for generating sequences of daily radiation values using a library of Markov transition matrices", Solar Energy 40(3):269-279 (1988): only monthly mean radiation is needed; serial dependence is confined to adjacent days; 10 matrices, each for a band of monthly mean clearness index Kt; daily Kt discretised into 10 states; fitted on ~300 months from 9 stations (Europe, Africa, Asia); synthetic series statistically indistinguishable from measured — [bibsonomy record](https://bibsonomy.org/bibtex/8620381d24811221f16513400997140e); [solaR calcG0 docs](https://github.com/cran/solaR/blob/master/man/calcG0.Rd); Meteonorm's daily generator descends from this method — [Meteonorm TMY process](https://docs.meteonorm.com/knowledge/climate/tmy-process)
- Reference implementation (R, solaR `markovG0`): monthly `ktm = G0dm / B0dm`; matrix index `findInterval(ktm, Ktm, all.inside=TRUE)`; daily state `s[i] ~ Categorical(MTM_month[s[i-1], :])`; daily `Kt = Uniform(class_lo[s], class_hi[s])` where the 10 classes are equal-width between `Ktlim[0,m]` and `Ktlim[1,m]`; finally rescale each month so the generated monthly mean equals the target (`G0d = Kt·B0d·G0dm/mean(Kt·B0d)`) — [solaR R/markovG0.R](https://github.com/cran/solaR/blob/master/R/markovG0.R) (GPL-3)
- Matrix library extracted from solaR `data/MTM.RData` (rows = previous-day state 1..10, columns = next-day state; values rounded to 3 decimals): `Ktm` thresholds = [0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45, 0.50, 0.55, 0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 1.00]; `Ktlim` (min, max daily Kt per matrix) = M1 (0.031, 0.705), M2 (0.058, 0.694), M3 (0.051, 0.753), M4 (0.052, 0.753), M5 (0.028, 0.807), M6 (0.053, 0.856), M7 (0.044, 0.818), M8 (0.085, 0.846), M9 (0.010, 0.842), M10 (0.319, 0.865) — [solaR data (CRAN mirror)](https://github.com/cran/solaR/tree/master/data)

### Inferences
- solaR maps monthly Kt < 0.35 to matrix 1 and Kt ≥ 0.70 to matrix 9 (`all.inside=TRUE` means M10 is never used); the original paper's class boundaries should be checked — this mapping looks shifted by one class relative to "M1 = Kt ≤ 0.30". Kyiv's Nov-Jan monthly Kt (~0.25-0.35) falls in the M1/M2 range, where transitions are noisy (few source months).
- Practical pipeline for solar_calc: (1) preferred — use 19 real years from PVGIS `seriescalc` and report LOLP per year plus worst year/worst 5-day spell; (2) offline/no data — generate N synthetic years with the Aguiar chain from monthly GHI (already built in), then build each day's 10-min profile as clear-sky × day-specific Kt scaling (the existing "clear sky × k" machinery can be reused per day); 100 synthetic years × 8760 h is still sub-second if the per-step model is kept simple (see Q11).
- Report LOLP / "days without full supply" with a distribution (median, P90) instead of a single number — averaging hides exactly what battery buyers in Ukraine (outage resilience) care about.

### Gaps
- The full text of Aguiar et al. (1988) was not accessed; exact class boundaries and the original author list (Conde) are confirmed only through secondary records. Hourly disaggregation (Aguiar & Collares-Pereira TAG model) was not researched in detail.
- No Ukraine-specific validation of the Markov library was found.

## Q3. Transposition: Perez vs Hay-Davies vs isotropic

### Takeaway
Switching the current "isotropic ×0.95" to Perez (1990 coefficients) raises annual plane-of-array irradiance by roughly +3 to +6% for 30-40° south tilts in diffuse-rich climates (more for steep/vertical panels); Hay-Davies captures about two-thirds of that gain with one line of code; both are trivially cheap in pure Python.

### Cited Findings
- **Perez (1990) as implemented in pvlib:** z = solar zenith (radians, apparent), κ = 1.041; sky brightness Δ = DHI·AM/E0 (AM = relative, not pressure-corrected); clearness ε = ((DHI+DNI)/DHI + κz³)/(1+κz³); ε bins with edges 1, 1.065, 1.23, 1.5, 1.95, 2.8, 4.5, 6.2; F1 = max(0, F11 + F12·Δ + F13·z), F2 = F21 + F22·Δ + F23·z; a = max(0, cos AOI), b = max(cos 85°, cos z); E_sky = DHI·[(1−F1)(1+cos β)/2 + F1·a/b + F2·sin β] (clipped ≥ 0) — [pvlib irradiance.py `perez`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/irradiance.py); Perez et al. 1990, Solar Energy 44(5):271-289
- **'allsitescomposite1990' coefficients** (rows = ε bins 1..8; columns F11 F12 F13 F21 F22 F23): bin1 −0.008 0.588 −0.062 −0.060 0.072 −0.022; bin2 0.130 0.683 −0.151 −0.019 0.066 −0.029; bin3 0.330 0.487 −0.221 0.055 −0.064 −0.026; bin4 0.568 0.187 −0.295 0.109 −0.152 −0.014; bin5 0.873 −0.392 −0.362 0.226 −0.462 0.001; bin6 1.132 −1.237 −0.412 0.288 −0.823 0.056; bin7 1.060 −1.600 −0.359 0.264 −1.127 0.131; bin8 0.678 −0.327 −0.250 0.156 −1.377 0.251 — [pvlib `_get_perez_coefficients`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/irradiance.py)
- pvlib warns that Perez has discontinuities between bins and offers the continuous **Perez-Driesse** variant (`perez_driesse`) — [pvlib irradiance.py](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/irradiance.py)
- **Hay-Davies:** AI = DNI/E0; Rb = max(cos AOI, 0)/max(cos z, 0.01745); E_sky = DHI·[(1−AI)(1+cos β)/2 + AI·Rb] — [pvlib `haydavies`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/irradiance.py)
- Empirical validation (Loutzenhiser et al. 2007, Dübendorf CH, vertical façade): isotropic mean absolute difference 13.7-14.9% vs 6.6-9.0% for Perez 1990 variants — [Strathprints record](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/5008/)
- Bolzano (2022 benchmarking): isotropic underestimates vertical global irradiance by 6-12% of daily peak — [unibz record](https://bia.unibz.it/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Benchmarking-clear-sky-and-transposition-models/991006249097801241)
- NREL notes isotropic POA can underestimate by 5-20% — [NREL 66545](https://nrel.gov/docs/fy17osti/66545.pdf)
- **[own computation]** Annual POA, south-facing, albedo 0.2, pvlib 0.16.1 (relative to isotropic): Sand Point 55N (DHI/GHI 0.56): tilt 15° Hay-Davies +1.8%, Perez +2.8%; **tilt 35° Hay-Davies +3.4%, Perez +5.5%**, Perez-Driesse +5.3%, Reindl +3.7%, Klucher +4.3%; tilt 60° HD +4.7%, Perez +7.6%; tilt 90° HD +5.3%, Perez +8.6%. Greensboro 36N (DHI/GHI 0.44): tilt 35° HD +2.4%, Perez +4.4%; tilt 90° HD +1.6%, Perez +5.2% — computed on [pvlib bundled TMY3 files](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)

### Inferences
- solar_calc currently multiplies isotropic sky diffuse by 0.95, i.e. it is *below* isotropic; vs Perez the combined under-prediction of annual POA for a Kyiv 35° south array is plausibly ~5-7%, larger in winter months when circumsolar/horizon brightening matter for battery autonomy. Use PVGIS DRcalc/seriescalc POA (which already includes PVGIS's own transposition) as a cross-check.
- Perez needs DNI and DHI separately at each step; with Erbs decomposition this is already available. The Perez bin lookup + 2 linear expressions cost a few dozen floating-point operations per step.
- Recommended: Perez 1990 (or Perez-Driesse to avoid jumps) as default; keep isotropic as a "conservative" option, drop the ×0.95 factor (if it was meant as a soiling/IAM proxy, model IAM for diffuse explicitly — see Q5).

### Gaps
- No published annual Perez-vs-isotropic figure specific to Kyiv / Central-Eastern Europe was found; the numbers above are own computations on non-European TMYs. Computing them for Kyiv requires one PVGIS `tmy` download (blocked here).

## Q4. Horizon profile shading and sky-view factor

### Takeaway
PVGIS `printhorizon` provides a free 360° terrain horizon (and `userhorizon` lets users enter their own); beam should be blocked when sun elevation < horizon(azimuth), and isotropic diffuse should be scaled by a precomputed obstruction-weighted sky-view factor — both cost almost nothing per time step once tabulated.

### Cited Findings
- `printhorizon` returns horizon height (degrees) at equidistant azimuths; pvlib converts azimuth to north=0/south=180 by adding 180° — [pvlib get_pvgis_horizon](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/iotools/pvgis.py)
- `userhorizon` is a list of heights starting North and moving clockwise, accepted by PVcalc/seriescalc/tmy — [pyPVGIS copy of JRC docs](https://github.com/canarionyc/pyPVGIS); [pvlib get_pvgis_hourly docstring](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/iotools/pvgis.py)
- For a horizontal surface with uniform horizon elevation h under isotropic sky, diffuse irradiance scales with cos²h; with multiple azimuth bins the SVF is the average of cos²h_i — [US patent text (sky-view factor as average of cos² terms)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11934750)
- Two competing SVF definitions: Dozier & Frew (1990) = fraction of isotropic sky irradiance received; Zakšek et al. (2011) = solid angle of visible sky; Rakovec & Zakšek propose a "diffuse tilt factor" that depends on ground reflectivity and diffuse/global ratio — [Renewable Energy 37 (2012) record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v37y2012i1p440-444.html)
- Urban-canyon study adapting transposition models with sky and circumsolar view factors found Perez with 35° circumsolar half-angle performed best — [Renewable Energy 180 (2021) record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v180y2021icp1194-1209.html)
- Row-to-row diffuse masking (if a user has multiple rows): average masking angle ψ̄(β, GCR) and diffuse loss = (1 − cos ψ̄)/2 per Passias & Källbäck — [pvlib shading.py `masking_angle_passias`, `sky_diffuse_passias`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/shading.py)

### Inferences
- Beam: `beam_visible = sun_elev > interp(horizon_profile, sun_azimuth)` (linear interpolation in azimuth, wrap at 360°); also zero the Perez circumsolar term when beam is blocked.
- Diffuse for a tilted plane (isotropic part): precompute once per configuration, on a sky grid (e.g. 5° azimuth × 2° elevation ≈ 1,600 cells), f_sky = Σ_visible max(0, cos θ_cell)·cos(e)·ΔeΔφ / Σ_all-above-0 max(0, cos θ_cell)·cos(e)·ΔeΔφ, where θ_cell is the angle between the panel normal and the cell direction and "visible" means e > h(φ). Multiply the isotropic term (and, approximately, the Perez horizon-band term) by f_sky. The blocked part of the sky could be replaced by the obstruction's own reflected radiance (albedo × obstruction illumination), a small refinement.
- PVGIS terrain horizons do not include nearby buildings/trees; the UI should let users override/merge with a measured profile (e.g., from a phone app), which PVGIS also accepts via `userhorizon`.

### Gaps
- PVGIS's own internal treatment of diffuse shading by the horizon was not documented in sources reached. No measured Ukraine data on yield loss from typical urban horizons was found.

## Q5. PV module model: power/low-light, temperature with wind, IAM, spectral, bifacial, degradation (TOPCon vs PERC, LID/LeTID)

### Takeaway
A full single-diode model is unnecessary for energy yield; the biggest cheap wins are (a) a wind-dependent cell temperature model (Faiman/PVsyst) instead of NOCT, (b) an empirical efficiency-vs-irradiance model (Huld/PVGIS or ADR) instead of an ad-hoc log factor, (c) correct IAM for diffuse, and (d) technology-specific first-year loss and degradation; spectral and bifacial effects are ≤±2-3% for a home rooftop.

### Cited Findings
**Cell temperature**
- **Faiman:** T_m = T_a + G_poa/(U0 + U1·WS), defaults U0 = 25.0 W/(m²K), U1 = 6.84 W·s/(m³K) (Faiman 2008, Prog. Photovolt. 16:307-315; 7 silicon modules) — [pvlib temperature.py `faiman`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/temperature.py)
- **PVsyst:** T_c = T_a + α·G·(1 − η_m)/(U_c + U_v·WS); α = 0.9; U_c = 29 (freestanding), 20 (semi-integrated), 15 (insulated/roof-integrated), U_v = 0 by default — [pvlib `pvsyst_cell` & TEMPERATURE_MODEL_PARAMETERS](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/temperature.py)
- **SAPM:** T_m = G·exp(a + b·WS) + T_a; T_c = T_m + (G/1000)·ΔT; (a, b, ΔT) = open_rack_glass_glass (−3.47, −0.0594, 3), close_mount_glass_glass (−2.98, −0.0471, 1), open_rack_glass_polymer (−3.56, −0.0750, 3), insulated_back_glass_polymer (−2.81, −0.0455, 0) — [pvlib temperature.py](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/temperature.py)
- **[own computation]** Annual DC yield (γ = −0.35%/K, Perez POA 35°) relative to NOCT-type model T_c = T_a + G/800·(45−20): Faiman +2.3-2.4%, PVsyst U_c=29 +1.1-1.5%, SAPM open-rack +1.5-1.6% (both TMY sites) — [pvlib bundled TMY3 data](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)

**Efficiency vs irradiance & temperature (low light)**
- **Huld model (used by PVGIS):** P = G′·P_stc·(1 + k1·ln G′ + k2·ln²G′ + k3·T′ + k4·T′·ln G′ + k5·T′·ln²G′ + k6·T′²), G′ = G_eff/1000, T′ = T_mod − 25. PVGIS 5 c-Si: k1..k6 = −0.017237, −0.040465, −0.004702, 0.000149, 0.000170, 0.000005; CIS: −0.005554, −0.038724, −0.003723, −0.000905, −0.001256, 0.000001; CdTe: −0.046689, −0.072844, −0.002262, 0.000276, 0.000159, −0.000006. **Updated ("PVGIS 6") c-Si: −0.0067560, −0.016444, −0.003015, −0.000045, −0.000043, 0.0** (Chatzipanagi et al., Prog. Photovolt. 33(8):905-917, 2025) — [pvlib pvarray.py `huld`/`_infer_k_huld`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/pvarray.py); Huld et al. 2011, Sol. Energy Mater. Sol. Cells 95:3359-3369
- PVGIS API now offers `pvtechchoice=crystSi2025` alongside `crystSi` — [pyPVGIS copy](https://github.com/canarionyc/pyPVGIS)
- **ADR model (Driesse & Stein):** s = G/1000, dT = T_c − 25; s_o = 10^(k_d + dT·tc_d), s_o,ref = 10^(k_d); v = ln(s/s_o + 1)/ln(1/s_o,ref + 1); η = k_a·[(1 + k_rs + k_rsh)·v − k_rs·s − k_rsh·v²]; example parameters k_a=1, k_d=−6.0, tc_d=0.02, k_rs=0.05, k_rsh=0.10 give relative efficiency 0.928 at 200 W/m² — [pvlib pvarray.py `pvefficiency_adr`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/pvarray.py); Driesse, Theristis & Stein, IEEE JPV 11(2):527-534 (2021)
- pvlib ships a CEC module library and an ADR-fitted CEC inverter library as CSV — [pvlib/data](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)
- **[own computation]** Share of annual POA energy arriving at G < 200 W/m²: 7.9% (Greensboro) vs 22.8% (Sand Point) — so low-light modelling matters much more for Kyiv-like climates. Huld PVGIS5 c-Si gave 3.0-3.9% less annual DC than a linear γ=−0.35%/K model; the 2025 updated coefficients gave 0.5-2.4% less (difference mixes low-light and the Huld implied temperature coefficient of ≈−0.47%/K) — [pvlib bundled TMY3 data](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)

**Incidence angle modifier**
- ASHRAE: IAM = 1 − b·(1/cos θ − 1), b = 0.05, negative values clipped to 0 — [pvlib iam.py `ashrae`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/iam.py)
- Martin-Ruiz: IAM = (1 − exp(−cos θ/a_r))/(1 − exp(−1/a_r)), default a_r = 0.16; companion `martin_ruiz_diffuse` gives IAM for sky-diffuse and ground-reflected components as functions of tilt — [pvlib iam.py](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/iam.py)
- Physical (Fresnel + absorption) IAM defaults n = 1.526, K = 4 m⁻¹, L = 2 mm; optional AR coating index — [pvlib iam.py `physical`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/iam.py)

**Spectral**
- JRC/Huld spectral factor (cloud-aware, needs only air mass and clear-sky index k_c): M = 1 + a1·(e^(−k_c) − e^(−1)) + a2·(k_c − 1) + a3·(AM − 1.5); multi-Si a = (0.00172, 0.000508, 0.00000357)/0.00348 ≈ (0.494, 0.146, 0.00103); CdTe a = (0.000643, 0.000130, 0.0000108)/0.001150; derived at JRC Ispra — [pvlib spectrum/mismatch.py `spectral_factor_jrc`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/spectrum/mismatch.py); Huld, Sample & Dunlop, 24th EU PVSEC 2009
- Other spectral models in pvlib needing precipitable water/AOD: First Solar, SAPM, Caballero, PVSPEC, Polo — [pvlib mismatch.py](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/spectrum/mismatch.py)

**Bifacial**
- Flush/low residential roofs: sources conflict — 0-2% (vendor rule of thumb), ~5% (vendor glossary), "low single digits" for low-clearance rooftop; recommended clearance 0.5-1.5 m for rear light — [SurgePV bifacial gain glossary](https://www.surgepv.com/glossary/bifacial-gain); [SurgePV bifacial design blog](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/bifacial-solar-panel-design)
- Fixed-tilt ground mounts at "normal" albedo 0.2-0.5: +5-15% specific yield — [Solar Builder / RatedPower](https://solarbuildermag.com/news/what-ratedpower-learned-after-running-1000-bifacial-solar-project-simulations-last-month/); Hamburg thesis: 4% for 2.3 m row spacing, 8% with white ground cover — [HAW Hamburg REPOSIT](https://reposit.haw-hamburg.de/handle/20.500.12738/9131)

**Degradation & first-year losses**
- Jordan & Kurtz review (~2,000 field rates): median **0.5%/yr**; mean ~0.8%/yr, ~80% below 1%/yr — [NREL 56690](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy13osti/56690.pdf); [NREL research hub](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/photovoltaic-degradation-rates-an-analytical-review-2/)
- Later meta-analysis (80 studies, 610 observations): median 0.94%/yr; cell technology, mounting and method significant; cold-climate, well-ventilated c-Si projected 47-year life — [Univ. Augsburg OPUS](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/121206)
- LeTID: up to 6% in commercial PERC (Fraunhofer CSP benchmark; 4 manufacturers >3%, two stable <1%); pv magazine cites 4-10% for susceptible modules vs 1-3% for classic LID; maximum after ~10 years in Germany — [Fraunhofer CSP](https://www.csp.fraunhofer.de/en/press/news/LeTID-Benchmark.html); [pv magazine 2019](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2019/06/15/the-weekend-read-is-perc-still-bankable)
- Kiwa PVEL 2024 scorecard: median combined LID+LETID 0.43% for 2023 BOMs, 0.3% for Ga-doped p-type PERC — [PVEL 2024 scorecard](https://2024modulescorecard.pvel.com/lidletid)
- TOPCon UV-induced degradation (Fraunhofer ISE, EPJ PV 17:15, 2026): after 60 kWh/m² UV (~1 year in Germany) + light stabilisation some modules showed ~0 loss, others up to 5%; lab UV tests slightly overestimate field loss; NREL PV Lifetime: an n-type TOPCon module showed a slight *increase* over the first 1.5 years, while one PERC bifacial design was among faster (> 1%/yr) degraders — [EPJ PV 10.1051/epjpv/2026007](https://www.epj-pv.org/10.1051/epjpv/2026007); [NREL PV Lifetime 2025 report](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/pv-lifetime-project-2025-nlr-annual-report/)
- Manufacturers typically claim −0.4%/yr for TOPCon (warranties), which Fraunhofer argues needs independent evaluation — [Fraunhofer publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/498875)

### Inferences
- Replace NOCT with Faiman (U0=25, U1=6.84) using wind from PVGIS/Open-Meteo (10 m wind; reduce to module height with a log-profile factor ~0.7-0.8, or keep 10 m and accept slight overestimation of cooling); offer roof-type presets via PVsyst U_c (29/20/15). For a flush roof-mount, PVsyst "semi_integrated/insulated" values give noticeably hotter cells than NOCT.
- Replace the custom "log low-light factor" with the Huld polynomial (choose the 2025 `crystSi2025`-style coefficients for modern modules), which also matches PVGIS so results can be validated against PVcalc output.
- Year-N energy: P_N = P_0·(1 − LID_first_year)·(1 − r_d)^(N−1); suggested presets (to be confirmed with datasheets): PERC LID+LeTID ~0.5-2% first year then 0.5%/yr; TOPCon ~0-1% first-year (UVID variable) then ~0.4%/yr. Single-diode / De Soto is only needed for the MPPT voltage-window check — the existing linear I-V approximation with temperature-corrected Voc/Vmp is adequate there.
- Bifacial (optional): G_rear ≈ ρ·GHI·(1 + cos(180°−β))/2·f_struct + DHI·(1 − cos β)/2, P gain = φ_bifaciality·G_rear/G_front, with f_struct ≈ 0.3-0.6 for low roof mounts; expect 0-3% on flush roofs, 5-10% on elevated ground racks over grass, higher over snow (albedo 0.6-0.8) — an inference consistent with the ranges above, not a sourced model.

### Gaps
- No source reached gave the PVGIS/JRC Faiman U0/U1 values (PVGIS uses its own pair; not verified), nor measured TOPCon vs PERC temperature coefficients and bifaciality factors — those should come from module datasheets.
- No single-diode parameter source or residential bifacial measurement for Ukraine was found. Wind-height correction for the Faiman model was not sourced.

## Q6. Snow losses (Marion/NREL and Townsend models) and Ukrainian winters

### Takeaway
Two pure-Python-friendly models exist: the hourly Marion (NREL/SAM) sliding-coverage model needing hourly snowfall (+optional snow depth), air temperature and POA; and the monthly Townsend model needing monthly snowfall, number of snow events, RH, temperature and POA; Open-Meteo provides the required snowfall/snow-depth inputs for Kyiv, but no measured Ukrainian snow-loss figures were found — literature suggests ~1-6% annual losses in moderate climates.

### Cited Findings
- **Marion et al. (2013) / NREL SAM coverage model:** module considered newly fully covered when hourly snowfall rate > 1 cm/h (and ground snow depth ≥ 1 cm if provided); snow slides when T_air > G_poa / m with m = −80 (W/m²/°C); slide per step = 0.197·sin(tilt)·Δt[h] (fraction of slant height); no sliding during snowfall; all snow slides if ground snow depth < 1 cm (Ryberg & Freeman 2017 improvement); coverage = 1 − cumulative slide since last snowfall, clipped at 0 — [pvlib snow.py `fully_covered_nrel`, `coverage_nrel`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/snow.py); Marion et al., Solar Energy 97:112-121 (2013); [NREL/TP-6A20-68705](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy17osti/68705.pdf)
- DC loss from coverage: loss = ceil(coverage·N_strings)/N_strings, where N_strings = number of cell strings in parallel along the slant height (a partially covered string loses its whole output; portrait 60-cell module → 1, landscape → 3) — [pvlib `dc_loss_nrel`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/snow.py); Gilman et al., NREL/TP-6A20-67399 (2018)
- **Townsend monthly model:** effective snow S_e = 0.5·S·(1 + 1/N) (S = monthly snowfall in inches, N = events); weighted S_e,w = (1/3)·S_e,prev + (2/3)·S_e; ground-interference γ = 2·H_slant·S_e,w·cos β·tan(θ_repose)/max(0.1, H_lower² − S_e,w²) (inches), θ_repose = 40°; loss fraction = C1·S_e,w·cos²β·(1 − C2·e^(−γ))·RH/T_air[K]²/POA_month[kWh/m²]^0.67, C1 = 5.7×10⁴, C2 = 0.51, clipped to [0,1] — [pvlib snow.py `loss_townsend`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/snow.py); Townsend & Powers, 37th IEEE PVSC 2011
- Moderate climates: typical annual snow losses ~5-6%, up to 9.3%; mild climates <2-3%; northern Sweden 263 sites: means 1.5-6.2%, up to 20% — [Energies 14(6):1574 (2021)](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/6/1574)
- Calumet, Michigan: 5-12% annual loss for elevated modules (steepest tilt least), 29-34% with ground interference — [MTU Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/materials_fp/85)
- Ground-mounted systems in Norway showed lower losses than previously documented roof-mounted ones; same-system year-to-year variation > 10 percentage points — [EPJ PV 2026 (Øgaard et al.)](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/full_html/2026/01/pv20250060/pv20250060.html)
- Kyiv snowfall (WeatherSpark climatology): Dec ~4.7″, Jan ~4.5″, Feb ~4.2″, Mar ~2.4″, Nov ~2.8″; snowy season ~28 Oct-5 Apr; stable snow cover increasingly absent (forecaster, Nov 2025) — [WeatherSpark Kyiv](https://weatherspark.com/y/96633/Average-Weather-in-Kiev-Ukraine-Year-Round); [Komersant UA](https://komersant.ua/en/pohoda-u-stolytsi-koly-u-kyievi-chekaty-na-snih-ta-morozy)
- Modelled Kyiv PV output ≈ 1.01 kWh/kWp/day in winter vs 6.50 in summer (NASA POWER based) — [Profile Solar Kyiv](https://profilesolar.com/locations/Ukraine/Kyiv/)

### Inferences
- Because Kyiv's Dec-Jan production is only ~15% of summer, even a 30-50% snow loss in those months is only ~1-3% of annual energy — but it can dominate winter battery autonomy/LOLP for off-grid or backup users, so snow should be modelled per day (Marion with Open-Meteo hourly snowfall/snow_depth, or a simpler "covered until a day with T_air > 0 °C and sun, then slide 0.197·sin β per sunny hour").
- Steep tilt (≥35-45°) and a free lower edge reduce losses; the UI should expose "clear snow manually" as an option.

### Gaps
- No measured snow-loss study for Ukraine or Poland/Germany with annual percentages was found (a 2025 EGU abstract analyses 129 Polish systems for 2023 but numbers were not available: [EGU25-6203](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-6203.html)). Kyiv snow-depth climatology was not found from an authoritative meteorological service.

## Q7. Inverter / MPPT efficiency and clipping

### Takeaway
A part-load efficiency curve (Sandia/CEC 6-parameter model, or the 1-parameter PVWatts curve) is easy to implement and captures the large low-load losses that a constant efficiency misses — important for winter mornings and for oversized hybrid inverters running small night loads.

### Cited Findings
- **Sandia (CEC) inverter model:** A = P_dco·(1 + C1·(V_dc − V_dco)), B = P_so·(1 + C2·(V_dc − V_dco)), C = C0·(1 + C3·(V_dc − V_dco)); P_ac = (P_aco/(A − B) − C·(A − B))·(P_dc − B) + C·(P_dc − B)²; then P_ac = min(P_ac, P_aco) (clipping) and P_ac = −|P_nt| when P_dc < P_so (night tare) — [pvlib inverter.py `sandia`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/inverter.py); King et al., SAND2007-5036
- Example CEC coefficients (SMA SB5.0-1SP-US-40 @240 V): P_so = 32.2 W, P_aco = 5050 W, P_dco = 5207 W, V_dco = 365 V, C0 = −3.27e-6 1/W, C1 = −3.6e-5 1/V, C2 = 1.76e-3 1/V, C3 = 1.15e-4 1/V, P_nt = 1.5 W; the library has ~3,260 inverters — [pvlib data sam-library-cec-inverters-2019-03-05.csv](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)
- **PVWatts inverter:** ζ = P_dc/P_dc0, P_dc0 = P_ac0/η_nom; η = (η_nom/η_ref)·(−0.0162·ζ − 0.0059/ζ + 0.9858), η_ref = 0.9637, η_nom default 0.96; P_ac = min(η·P_dc, P_ac0), ≥ 0 — [pvlib inverter.py `pvwatts`](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/inverter.py); Dobos, NREL/TP-6A20-62641 (2014)
- **Weighted efficiencies** to calibrate a curve from a datasheet: η_EU = 0.03η5 + 0.06η10 + 0.13η20 + 0.10η30 + 0.48η50 + 0.20η100 (JRC Ispra climate); η_CEC = 0.04η10 + 0.05η20 + 0.12η30 + 0.21η50 + 0.53η75 + 0.05η100 — [Open Energy Modelling wiki](https://wiki.openmod-initiative.org/wiki/European_Efficiency); [DNV SolarFarmer inverter model](https://myworkspace.dnv.com/download/public/renewables/solarfarmer/manuals/v1_1/CalcRef/Theory/InverterEfficiencyModel.html)
- EN 50530 defines static and dynamic MPPT efficiency and an "overall" efficiency = static MPPT × conversion efficiency; Euro/CEC figures cover conversion only — [MDPI Energies 15(8):2857](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/15/8/2857)
- Inverters typically operate at 20-40% of rated power under non-arid conditions — [Paderborn microinverter paper](https://ei.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/elektrotechnik/fg/nek/PDF/Presented__at_EU_PVSEC_2022_MICROINVERTER_PV_SYSTEMS.pdf)

### Inferences
- A pure-Python fit from three datasheet numbers (self-consumption P_0, η_max, η at 100%) to the loss model P_loss = P_0 + k1·P_out + k2·P_out² is equivalent in shape to the Sandia quadratic; use separate curves for PV→AC, PV→battery (MPPT charger), battery→AC (inverter) paths in hybrid units.
- Clipping must be computed at the 10-min (or finer) step, not on averages; average-day profiles underestimate clipping on clear days and overestimate it on cloudy ones.
- Off-grid hybrid inverters commonly have 30-100 W idle draw; with a 24 h idle this is 0.7-2.4 kWh/day, comparable to winter PV yield of small systems — keep the existing idle term and make it power-dependent.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found for MPPT efficiency at very low input power (<5% of rating) for residential MPPT charge controllers, nor for EU/Chinese hybrid inverter CEC-style coefficients (the CEC database is US-market only).

## Q8. Battery models: LiFePO4 CC-CV, OCV, temperature, efficiency, aging; lead-acid Peukert/KiBaM

### Takeaway
For energy simulation, an energy bucket with (i) separate charge/discharge efficiencies, (ii) a CV-taper current limit near full, (iii) temperature-based charge-current limits, and (iv) a semi-empirical calendar+cycle aging model (BLAST-Lite LFP equations) is sufficient and fast; KiBaM is the standard two-tank refinement for lead-acid (rate-dependent capacity).

### Cited Findings
**CC-CV, OCV, temperature (mostly vendor-grade sources)**
- CC to ~3.65 V/cell, then CV until current tapers to ~0.05C (some guides 0.05-0.10C); CC-only charge reaches ~97%, CC-CV 100% — [Relion charging instructions](https://www.coastalclimatecontrol.com/images/PDF/ReLiON/Relion_Charging_Instructions_v3.pdf); [lifepo4.com.au charging guide](https://lifepo4.com.au/lifepo4-long-life-charging-guide/)
- Resting OCV values differ between vendors: ~3.20 V/cell at 20% SOC and ~3.33 V at 80% in one table; 3.05-3.15 V at 20% and 3.40-3.45 V at 100% in another; flat plateau makes voltage-based SOC unreliable — [Wevolver LiFePO4 chart](https://www.wevolver.com/article/lifepo4-voltage-chart-soc-voltages-for-32v12v24v48v-systems); [KAIST study](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/288264)
- Charging below 0 °C must be avoided (lithium plating) per REDARC; Relion allows −20…55 °C charging but reduced to 0.1C (0…−10 °C) and 0.05C (−10…−20 °C); many BMS block cold charging — [Why not charge lithium below 0 °C (REDARC)](https://cdn2.shopify.com/s/files/1/2204/8993/files/Why_You_Should_not_charge_a_lithium_battery_below_0.pdf?438); [Relion instructions](https://www.coastalclimatecontrol.com/images/PDF/ReLiON/Relion_Charging_Instructions_v3.pdf)
- Round-trip efficiency figures: Victron LFP 92% (full cycle); system-level 92-95% (Zendure); SunPower residential LFP >86%; Safoutin et al. (SAE 2015-01-1186) measured an A123 LFP pack at 0.2-2C and 10-90% SOC (numbers not retrieved) — [Victron via Lindemann](https://lindemann-kg.de/en/products/6/Batterien/Lithium-Eisen-Phosphat%20Batterien/Victron%20Energy); [Zendure](https://zendure.com/blogs/news/solar-battery-efficiency-rte-guide); [SAE 2015-01-1186](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2015-01-1186)

**Aging: NREL BLAST-Lite LFP-Gr 250 Ah prismatic (2019 cell) model**
- Calendar rate k_cal = |p1|·exp(p2/T)·exp(p3·U_a/T); cycle rate k_cyc = (p4 + p5·DOD + p6·C_rate)·(exp(p7/T) + exp(−p8/T)); T in K; p1 = 8.37e4, p2 = −5.21e3, p3 = −3.56e3, p_cal = 0.526, p4 = 4.38e-8, p5 = 1.55e-8, p6 = 1.68e-7, p7 = 2.19e3, p8 = 1.55e5, p_cyc = 0.828; capacity loss trajectories q_loss,t = k_cal·t_days^p_cal and q_loss,EFC = k_cyc·EFC^p_cyc (EFC = equivalent full cycles), integrated incrementally as dy = k·p·(y/k)^((p−1)/p)·dx; relative capacity q = 1 − q_loss,t − q_loss,EFC; validity: 10-45 °C cycling, DOD 0.8-1, ≤0.65C charge, ≤1C discharge — [BLAST-Lite lfp_gr_250AhPrismatic_2019.py](https://github.com/NREL/BLAST-Lite/blob/main/blast/models/lfp_gr_250AhPrismatic_2019.py); [degradation_model.py](https://github.com/NREL/BLAST-Lite/blob/main/blast/models/degradation_model.py); test data: [doi 10.1016/j.est.2023.109042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.est.2023.109042)
- Anode potential vs SOC used as stressor (Safari & Delacourt 2011): X_a = 0.0085 + SOC·(0.78 − 0.0085); U_a = 0.6379 + 0.5416·e^(−305.5309·X_a) + 0.044·tanh(−(X_a − 0.1958)/0.1088) − 0.1978·tanh((X_a − 1.0571)/0.0854) − 0.6875·tanh((X_a + 0.0117)/0.0529) − 0.0175·tanh((X_a − 0.5692)/0.0875) — [BLAST-Lite degradation_model.py `_get_Ua`](https://github.com/NREL/BLAST-Lite/blob/main/blast/models/degradation_model.py)
- BLAST-Lite uses rainflow cycle counting (`blast/utils/rainflow.py`), is BSD-3-licensed, and its README warns models are fit on 1-3 years of accelerated tests and do not cover overcharge/abuse/cell imbalance — [BLAST-Lite repo](https://github.com/NREL/BLAST-Lite)
- Simple Wöhler alternative: N(DOD) = a·DOD^(−b) with Miner's rule damage D = Σ n_i/N_i and calendar cap (BREOS "Polysun-style" defaults a = 5000, b = 1.6, calendar 20 y — software defaults, not an LFP calibration); LFP literature disagrees on whether deeper cycles are more harmful per kWh — [BREOS polysun_degradation](https://breos.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/generated/breos.polysun_degradation.html); [DLR study](https://elib.dlr.de/140070); [arXiv 2011.14903](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.14903)

**Lead-acid / rate effects**
- **KiBaM (Manwell & McGowan 1993, HOMER):** two tanks — available q1 (fraction c of capacity) and bound q2; dq1/dt = −I + k·(h2 − h1), dq2/dt = −k·(h2 − h1), h1 = q1/c, h2 = q2/(1 − c); battery "empty" when q1 = 0. HOMER max discharge power over Δt: P_max = [k·Q1·e^(−kΔt) + Q·k·c·(1 − e^(−kΔt))]/[1 − e^(−kΔt) + c·(kΔt − 1 + e^(−kΔt))] (Q1 available, Q total energy, k in 1/h), then × discharge efficiency — [HOMER Pro kinetic battery model](https://support.ul-renewables.com/homer-manuals-pro/kinetic_battery_model.html); [HOMER max discharge power](https://support.homerenergy.com/docs/results/calculations/maximum-battery-discharge-power); [arXiv 1604.00190](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00190); UMass KiBaM tool — [UMass](https://www.umass.edu/windenergy/research/topics/tools/software/kibam/)
- **Peukert:** t = H·(C/(I·H))^k (H = rated discharge time, C = rated capacity, k = Peukert exponent; lead-acid typically 1.1-1.3, Li-ion close to 1) — [Wikipedia: Peukert's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peukert%27s_law)

### Inferences
- Implementable LFP model for solar_calc (per 10-min step): η_ch ≈ η_dis ≈ √RTE (RTE 0.92-0.95 for LFP cells+BMS; add inverter/charger losses separately); CV taper: I_ch,max = I_cc for SOC < SOC_cv, else I_cc·(1 − SOC)/(1 − SOC_cv) with SOC_cv ≈ 0.90-0.95 and stop at I < 0.05C; temperature: charge current 0 below 0 °C (or 0.1C/0.05C bands if the battery datasheet allows), discharge capacity factor vs temperature (existing "cold capacity factor"); battery temperature should be indoor/room temperature with thermal inertia, not outdoor air, unless installed outside.
- OCV curve is only needed if the program models battery voltage (for MPPT window/inverter low-voltage cutoffs); a 10-point lookup table from the chosen battery's datasheet is enough.
- Aging: run BLAST-Lite-style equations once per day using daily stressors (mean T, mean SOC → U_a, daily EFC, DOD from rainflow or max−min SOC); this costs microseconds per day and gives capacity fade over 10-20 years for economics (Q10). Clearly label as "expected life under nominal conditions" per the BLAST-Lite caveats.
- Lead-acid: use KiBaM (2 states) with Peukert-consistent parameters; it is cheap and captures "can't deliver high power at low SOC".

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed LFP OCV-SOC table or measured RTE-vs-C-rate dataset was retrieved (SAE paper numbers and Data-in-Brief dataset not accessed). No calibrated LFP Wöhler exponent with uncertainty was found. BLAST-Lite LFP models are cell-level; pack/BMS losses are not included.

## Q9. Load profiles (BDEW H0/H25, appliance-based), motor start surges

### Takeaway
The BDEW standard household profiles (new 2025 H25, plus P25 for PV homes and S25 for PV+battery homes) are freely usable via oemof demandlib (MIT) as small 15-min tables, with a 4th-order "dynamisation" polynomial for seasonality; no Ukrainian standard profile was found, so an appliance-based builder plus H25 shape is the pragmatic approach, with compressor start surges of ~3-8× running current for inverter sizing.

### Cited Findings
- demandlib implements BDEW 2025 profiles: **G25** (commerce, static, 2022-23 data), **H25** (household, dynamic, 2018-19 data), **L25** (farm), **P25** (households with PV, dynamic, 2022-23), **S25** (households with battery storage, dynamic, 2022-23); MIT licence; values scaled to 1 GWh/year at 15-min resolution — [demandlib bdew/_profiles25.py](https://github.com/oemof/demandlib/blob/dev/src/oemof/demand/bdew/_profiles25.py)
- H25 table layout: 96 quarter-hour rows × 12 months × 3 day types (SA = Saturday, FT = Sunday/holiday, WT = working day), e.g. January 00:00-00:15 = 22.152 / 23.148 / 20.126 — [demandlib bdew_data/h25.csv](https://github.com/oemof/demandlib/tree/dev/src/oemof/demand/bdew/bdew_data)
- Dynamisation (H0/H25): F(t) = −3.92e-10·t⁴ + 3.2e-7·t³ − 7.02e-5·t² + 2.1e-3·t + 1.24, t = day of year; demandlib uses −3.916649251e-10 for exact normalisation in the legacy H0 implementation — [demandlib elec_slp.py](https://github.com/oemof/demandlib/blob/dev/src/oemof/demand/bdew/elec_slp.py)
- Motor starting: direct-on-line starting (locked-rotor) current ≈ 5-8× full-load current; first-cycle peak can be ~2.8× the RMS LRA; sizing guidance: compressor nameplate LRA × voltage + ~20% margin; refrigerator surge claims range from ~3× running (mini fridge, 280-360 W) to 800-1,500 W for 2-5 s (vendor sources, conflicting) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/motor-inrush-current-vs-locked-rotor-amps-the-difference); [Renogy](https://www.renogy.com/blog/what-size-generator-do-i-need-to-run-a-refrigerator); [Anker SOLIX](https://www.ankersolix.com/blogs/portable-power-station/what-size-solar-generator-to-run-refrigerator)

### Inferences
- Build loads as: (a) H25 (or P25/S25) shape scaled to user's annual kWh, or (b) appliance list (power, hours, time window, duty cycle) summed onto the same 10-min grid; use H25 for the "background" residual. Store H25 as a 12×3×96 table (~3.5k numbers) in the program.
- Surge check: per appliance, store `surge_factor` (resistive 1×, LED/electronics 1-2× brief, fixed-speed compressor/pump 3-8× for ~0.1-3 s, inverter compressors ~1-1.5×) and test max(Σ running + max single surge) vs inverter surge rating — a check, not part of the energy simulation.
- Ukrainian households differ from German H25 (electric heating/boilers, induction cooking, outage-driven behaviour); allow seasonal scaling by the user.

### Gaps
- No official Ukrainian residential standard load profile (e.g., from Ukrenergo/NEURC) was found. No authoritative standard for fridge surge magnitude/duration was found (vendor numbers only). The P25/S25 profiles' documentation was not read in detail.

## Q10. Economics: payback, NPV, LCOE, self-consumption and self-sufficiency

### Takeaway
Use the standard load-matching definitions (self-consumption = self-used PV / total PV; self-sufficiency = self-used PV / total load) and discounted cash-flow metrics with degradation and battery fade included; batteries of 0.5-1 kWh per kWp typically raise self-consumption by 13-24 percentage points.

### Cited Findings
- Self-consumption = share of total PV production directly consumed by the owner (Luthander et al., Applied Energy 142:80-94, 2015); battery 0.5-1 kWh/kWp raises relative self-consumption by 13-24 percentage points, demand-side management by 2-15 points — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/appene/v142y2015icp80-94.html)
- Self-sufficiency = locally generated energy consumed on site / total consumption (same numerator as self-consumption, denominator = load) — [Chalmers paper](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/248147/file/248147_Fulltext.pdf); TATuP uses Luthander's metric at 15-min resolution — [TATuP article](https://www.tatup.de/index.php/tatup/en/article/download/6968/11730/12477?inline=1)
- The common self-sufficiency formula breaks down (can leave 0-100%) when storage exchanges energy with the grid (grid charging) — [DTU paper](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/281004474/Re_thinking_the_definition_of_self_sufficiency.pdf)
- PVGIS PVcalc offers built-in LCOE inputs `pvprice`, `systemcost`, `interest` [%/yr], `lifetime` (default 25 y) — [pyPVGIS copy](https://github.com/canarionyc/pyPVGIS)
- NREL simple LCOE: LCOE = (overnight capital cost × CRF + fixed O&M)/(8760 × capacity factor) + variable O&M, CRF = i(1+i)^n/((1+i)^n − 1) — [NREL LCOE documentation](https://www.nrel.gov/analysis/tech-lcoe-documentation.html)

### Inferences
- Formulas to implement (annual steps t = 1..n, discount rate r, energy price escalation e):
  - Energy in year t: E_t = E_1·(1 − d)^(t−1) (PV) and usable battery capacity from the aging model (Q8).
  - Savings_t = E_self,t·p_buy·(1+e)^(t−1) + E_export,t·p_feed,t (Ukrainian "active consumer"/net-billing rules as input) + avoided generator fuel/outage cost (optional, important for Ukraine).
  - NPV = −I_0 + Σ (Savings_t − O&M_t − Replacement_t)/(1+r)^t; IRR = r such that NPV = 0; simple payback = I_0/Savings_1; discounted payback = first t where cumulative discounted cash flow ≥ 0.
  - LCOE = (I_0 + Σ (O&M_t + Repl_t)/(1+r)^t) / Σ E_t/(1+r)^t; for storage, levelised cost of stored energy uses Σ E_discharged,t in the denominator.
  - Self-consumption SC = E_self/E_PV; self-sufficiency SS = E_self/E_load, where E_self counts PV used directly + PV routed via battery (exclude grid-charged battery energy).
- Report all metrics from the same time-series simulation (with real or Markov weather), not from average days, since SC/SS with batteries are strongly nonlinear in day-to-day variability.

### Gaps
- The full Luthander 2015 text (exact SS formula) and NREL LCOE page were not fetched directly (snippets/known URL only). Current Ukrainian tariff/net-billing parameters were out of scope and not researched.

## Q11. Implementation priorities, accuracy impact and speed budget (pure Python)

### Takeaway
All recommended models fit easily in a sub-second pure-Python year: a full 10-minute year (52,560 steps) with Perez transposition, Faiman temperature and a bucket battery ran in 0.12 s in a plain Python loop; the highest-impact upgrades are real/synthetic day-to-day weather, Perez transposition and wind-aware temperature, followed by inverter part-load curves, snow, and battery taper/aging.

### Cited Findings
- **[own computation]** Pure-Python (no numpy) loop over 365×144 steps with Perez 1990 coefficients, Faiman temperature, linear power and a bucket battery: **0.117 s** on this session's container — script in session scratchpad (not saved to repo); coefficients from [pvlib irradiance.py](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/blob/main/pvlib/irradiance.py)
- Impact summary from own computations above: average-day vs real hourly weather changed unmet-load from 0% to 1-9% (warm site) and from ~23% to ~24-32% (cloudy site); Perez vs isotropic +4.4…+5.5% POA at 35°; Faiman vs NOCT +2.3…+2.4% DC energy; Huld vs linear −0.5…−3.9% DC energy — [pvlib bundled TMY3 data](https://github.com/pvlib/pvlib-python/tree/main/pvlib/data)
- Literature ranges: snow 1.5-6% annual in moderate/cold climates — [Energies 14(6):1574](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/6/1574); LeTID up to 6% (PERC) — [Fraunhofer CSP](https://www.csp.fraunhofer.de/en/press/news/LeTID-Benchmark.html); bifacial 0-3% (flush roofs) to 5-15% (ground) — [Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/news/what-ratedpower-learned-after-running-1000-bifacial-solar-project-simulations-last-month/)

### Inferences
- Suggested order (benefit/complexity): (1) hourly multi-year PVGIS `seriescalc`/`tmy` input + optional Aguiar Markov generator, simulate 8,760 h (or 10-min with interpolation) instead of 12 average days — largest effect on battery/LOLP/self-consumption; (2) Perez (or Perez-Driesse) + IAM for diffuse, drop ×0.95; (3) Faiman/PVsyst temperature with wind; Huld efficiency; (4) full horizon profile + SVF; (5) PVWatts/Sandia inverter curve with clipping and idle; MPPT efficiency vs power; (6) battery CV taper, cold-charge limits, KiBaM for lead-acid; daily BLAST-style aging; (7) snow (Marion, with Open-Meteo snowfall); (8) degradation/first-year loss presets; spectral (JRC factor, ±1-2%) and bifacial last.
- Keep the clear-sky model for gap-filling and for Markov day construction; Ineichen-Perez with monthly Linke turbidity (pvlib ships `LinkeTurbidities.h5`, but that file needs h5py) would be more accurate than Haurwitz — a small monthly Linke table for Kyiv could be embedded instead.
- Cache downloaded data per location (JSON) so the app works offline and respects PVGIS/Open-Meteo limits; never call APIs from inside the simulation loop.

### Gaps
- Own computations used US TMY3 sites rather than Kyiv because PVGIS/Open-Meteo were unreachable from this environment; re-running the same comparisons on a PVGIS Kyiv TMY is recommended before quoting Kyiv-specific percentages to users.
